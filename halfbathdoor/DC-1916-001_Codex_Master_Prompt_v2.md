# 1. PROJECT OBJECTIVE

Create a concise, practical, professionally designed, fully illustrated woodworking manual that allows a competent intermediate woodworker to build one historically appropriate five-panel interior door for a 1916 Dutch Colonial Revival residence.

The completed door must have the appearance, proportions, material character, panel arrangement, hardware style, and millwork relationships appropriate to an early twentieth-century interior.

The internal construction may use modern methods when those methods:

- Improve reliability.
- Reduce fabrication difficulty.
- Suit a well-equipped home workshop.
- Remain concealed in the finished door.
- Do not alter the historically appropriate appearance.
- Do not create obvious modern visual details.
- Produce a durable, repairable, and structurally sound door.

The primary modern joinery method shall be concealed loose-tenon joinery made with a Festool Domino DF 500.

The publication must distinguish clearly between:

- Historically appropriate appearance.
- Historically documented construction.
- Modern concealed construction methods.

Do not describe the door as an exact museum reproduction unless historical evidence supports that claim.

Use the term:

“Historically appropriate reproduction door using modern concealed joinery.”

# 5. MODERN CONSTRUCTION STANDARD

The door shall use concealed modern construction methods while preserving traditional appearance.

## Primary frame joinery

Use Festool Domino DF 500 loose-tenon joinery.

Do not use integral mortise-and-tenon joinery as the primary method.

Do not require:

- Router-table cope-and-stick tooling.
- Shaper tooling.
- CNC machining.
- Industrial door clamps.
- Large hollow-chisel mortiser.
- Dedicated commercial door-making machinery.

The primary construction must be executable using the listed home-shop equipment.

## Modern concealed methods permitted

- Multiple Domino loose tenons.
- Factory Festool tenons.
- Shop-made loose tenons when accurately manufactured.
- Modern PVA adhesive.
- Modern dust extraction.
- Precision story sticks.
- Digital calipers.
- Router templates.
- Track-guided or jig-guided machining.
- Elastomeric panel spacers where appropriate.
- Modern abrasives.
- Rubio Monocoat finish.
- Temporary steel screws before installing brass screws.
- Modern drill guides and centering bits.
- Laser or digital layout tools as optional aids.

## Methods not permitted in the primary build

- Pocket screws as structural door joinery.
- Biscuits as primary structural joinery.
- Brad nails as structural joinery.
- Screws through visible stile faces.
- Plywood panels presented as solid historical panels.
- MDF structural door components.
- Applied molding used to disguise structurally unsuitable construction.
- Permanently glued solid panels.
- Construction that prevents seasonal movement.

## Joint-design principles

Use the largest practical DF 500 tenon arrangement that:

- Fits within the stock.
- Leaves adequate material around the mortise.
- Does not weaken the panel groove.
- Does not break through the show face.
- Does not cause mortises to merge unintentionally.
- Can be accurately registered.
- Provides adequate resistance to sag and racking.

Wider rails may require multiple tenons.

Do not automatically use the same pattern for every rail.

Give additional structural consideration to:

- Bottom rail.
- Lock rail.

These joints may require more tenons than the top or intermediate rails.

The center muntin may require a different joint arrangement due to its narrower width.

# 13. TIGHT AND LOOSE MORTISE STRATEGY

Where multiple Domino mortises are used in one joint:

- At least one mortise must be cut at the tight setting to establish precise alignment.
- Additional mortises may use the wider setting only where assembly clearance is beneficial.
- Do not cut every mortise loose.
- Do not use loose settings to compensate for inaccurate layout.
- Do not depend on clamp pressure to correct registration errors.

Document the adopted strategy for each joint family.

- Whether paired mortises straddle the groove.
- Whether the groove passes between mortises.
- Whether any mortise intersects the groove.
- Whether groove termination is required.
- Whether a stopped groove is useful.
- Whether the rail shoulder needs a short groove closure.
- Whether the mortise arrangement leaves sufficient material.

- Operation number.

- Feed direction.

- Workholding.
- Dust extraction.
- Operation-specific safety notes.

Use numbered, chronological, executable steps.

Each step must explain:

- Workpiece orientation.
- Reference face.
- Reference edge.
- Which end is cut first.
- How the machine is registered.
- What test is performed.
- What measurement is checked.
- What acceptable fit looks like.
- What material may be adjusted.
- What material must not be adjusted.
- What condition requires remaking the part.

## Quality-control checklist

Use measurable acceptance criteria.

## Common problems

For each problem include:

- Symptom.
- Likely cause.
- Corrective action.
- Whether the part is salvageable.
- When the part must be remade.

## Cabinetmaker’s note

Include one concise practical insight.

## Stop condition

State exactly what must be correct before the next operation begins.

# 29. DIMENSIONAL VALIDATION

Create scripts that verify:

- Door width equals stile widths plus rail shoulder length.
- Door height equals the complete rail-and-opening stack.
- Rail blank length equals shoulder length plus both end allowances.
- Panel-opening dimensions reconcile.
- Panel dimensions reconcile with groove depth and movement allowances.
- Domino mortises fit within each component.
- Domino mortises do not break through faces.
- Domino mortises maintain required edge distances.
- Multiple mortises maintain minimum spacing.
- Domino mortises do not conflict structurally with panel grooves.
- Muntin joints fit within the muntin width.
- Board-foot totals reconcile.
- Rough-stock allowances are included.
- Cut-list quantities match component quantities.
- Moulding cut-list quantities reconcile with the completed jamb dimensions entered by the user.

Validation must fail when dimensions conflict.
