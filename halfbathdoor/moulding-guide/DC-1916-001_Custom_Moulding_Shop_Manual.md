# DC-1916-001 Custom Moulding Shop Manual

This companion manual covers the quarter-sawn white-oak door stop, casing, backband, and evidence-supported head-cap or plinth treatment around the completed jamb. The jamb is already installed and is not part of this fabrication scope.

## Scope and default appearance

- Material: FAS quarter-sawn white oak, selected to match the door and adjacent house millwork.
- Casing: 3/4 x 4-1/2 inches, flat, with visible arrises eased about 1/32 inch.
- Backband: 1 x 1-1/8 inches, edge-applied outside the casing, with its face 1/4 inch proud.
- Door stop: 3/8 x 1-1/4 inches, flat, with the exposed arris eased about 1/16 inch.
- Reveal: 3/16 inch from the inside jamb edge to the inside casing edge.
- Head backband M-202C: mandatory on every trimmed face; it completes the three-piece backband surround.
- Optional head cap: 7/8 inch thick x 1-1/2 inches deep, seated atop the completed head backband with 3/4-inch overhang beyond that fitted backband at each end.
- Optional plinth blocks: use only when adjacent original trim supports them; all dimensions remain field controlled.
- Base cut-list quantities cover one trimmed face plus one three-piece door-stop set. Duplicate casing, backband, cap, and plinth quantities when both faces receive new trim. Do not duplicate the stop set.

Required workflow: dry-fit the complete trim composition with temporary tacks only, label every part and its opening location, remove all parts, complete final P120 preparation and the approved finish, allow the finish to reach full cure, and only then return for permanent installation. Install evidence-supported plinths first as the casing bottom datum, then casing, mandatory backband and self-returns, optional cap, and the three-piece door stop last.

## Field release gate

1. Photograph and measure adjacent original casing, backband, stop, baseboard, head treatment, plinths, corners, and finish.
2. Record the number of faces receiving new trim.
3. Choose one head-joint scheme from house evidence: a mitered surround or a square-butt head. Do not mix schemes on the same face.
4. Measure the completed jamb opening width at top, middle, and bottom; measure height at both sides.
5. Record each jamb leg separately for plumb in the opening plane and plumb in the wall-normal plane. Record the head for level.
6. Measure both opening diagonals from matching jamb corners, record their difference, and confirm square rather than inferring it from width and height alone.
7. Place winding sticks across the jamb faces at the head, midpoint, and bottom; sight and record face twist. A twisted jamb or inconsistent face plane is a repair or extension-jamb design condition, not a casing-fastener correction.
8. Measure jamb projection relative to the wall at the head, both upper corners, both midpoints, and both lower corners.
9. Check wall flatness across the complete trim footprint with a straightedge.
10. Record the finished-floor condition, baseboard interface, inside and outside corners, and any utility risk in the intended fastener paths.
11. Stop before milling if adjacent trim evidence conflicts with the default profile, if the jamb or wall requires repair, or if cap and plinth use remains undecided.

## Measurement variables

| Symbol | Field measurement |
|---|---|
| W | Clear jamb width measured at the head where the casing reveal will be laid out |
| H-L / H-R | Face-specific vertical distance on each side from that face's selected bottom datum to the underside of the head jamb |
| R | Casing reveal; default 3/16 inch |
| C | Casing width; default 4-1/2 inches |
| B | Backband width; default 1-1/8 inches |
| P | Head-cap end overhang; default 3/4 inch |

Record H-L and H-R separately for each trimmed face when floor, plinth, or baseboard conditions differ. The selected bottom datum is the finished floor when no plinth is used, or the verified plinth top when a plinth is used.

## Length formulas

All formulas establish a rough target only. Add at least 2 inches of total field-fit allowance and transfer final cuts from the actual opening or fitted parts.

| Configuration | Controlled finished dimension |
|---|---|
| Mitered head casing, inside short point | W + 2R |
| Mitered head casing, outside long point | W + 2R + 2C |
| Mitered side casing, inside short point | H-L + R or H-R + R |
| Mitered side casing, outside long point | H-L + R + C or H-R + R + C |
| Square-butt side casing | H-L + R or H-R + R |
| Square-butt head casing | W + 2R + 2C |
| Mitered side backband, outside long point | Actual fitted mitered side-casing outside long point + B |
| Square-butt side backband | Actual fitted square-butt side-casing length + C |
| Head backband, outside long-point target | Actual fitted head-casing outside width + 2B |
| Optional head cap | Actual fitted head-backband outside width + 2P |

M-202C is mandatory on each trimmed face. Backband and stop final lengths are transferred from the fitted casing and closed door, not cut from nominal arithmetic. Fit M-204 only after the complete backband is fitted; its rough blank is the actual fitted head-backband outside width + 2P + 2 inches total allowance.

## Two-face moulding purchase order

This purchase order is separate from the owner-labeled A-O door stock. It covers new casing and backband on both faces of the opening plus one three-piece door-stop set. It uses the owner-confirmed 24 x 81-inch finished jamb opening only to establish conservative purchasing lengths; the completed-jamb worksheet still controls every final cut. The fitted slab is 23-3/4 x 80-1/2 inches and does not change these jamb-derived moulding quantities. Plinth blocks are not included because their presence, profile, width, and height require field evidence.

The correct lumber notation is **S4S 4/4 x nominal 6 inches x 8 feet**: surfaced four sides, four-quarter thickness class, nominal 6-inch width, and nominal 8-foot length. It is not “S4 4x4.” Require FAS quarter-sawn white oak, compatible color and ray figure, and at least 96 inches of actual board length.

| Qty | Dealer description | Minimum actual acceptance gate | Allocation |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 7 | S4S 4/4 quarter-sawn white oak, nominal 6 inches x 8 feet | At least 3/4 inch thick at the thinnest point, 5-1/2 inches wide, and 96 inches long; if exactly 3/4 inch thick, it must already be flat because no replaning allowance remains | Four side-casing boards; one board for both head casings; one full-length casing contingency/match board; one wide stop mother blank |
| 2 | S4S 6/4 quarter-sawn white oak, nominal 6 inches x 8 feet | At least 1-1/4 inches thick, 5-1/2 inches wide, and 96 inches long | One board per face for two side backbands plus one head backband; the remainder can also yield one optional head cap per face when a square-head treatment is supported |
| 9 total | Separate two-face moulding package | Approximately 40 dealer board feet: 28 BF of 4/4 plus 12 BF of 6/4 | Both faces, one stop set, and one full-length casing contingency; no plinth stock |

Four-quarter S4S stock is normally about 3/4 inch thick and therefore cannot produce the specified 1-inch-finished M-202 backband. Five-quarter S4S is often exactly 1 inch thick and may leave no cleanup allowance. The 6/4 stock must measure at least 1-1/4 inches after surfacing so the backband can finish at a true 1 inch; it also provides the thickness required for an optional 7/8-inch-finished M-204 head cap.

The 8-foot order accommodates either supported head-joint scheme:

| Head scheme | Four side-casing rough blanks | Four side-backband rough blanks | Minimum sound board length, including 2-inch end-cleanup reserve |
|---|---|---|---|
| Square-butt | 83-3/16 inches each | 87-11/16 inches each | Casing 85-3/16; backband 89-11/16 |
| Mitered | 87-11/16 inches each | 88-13/16 inches each | Casing 89-11/16; backband 90-13/16 |

For either scheme, crosscut two head casings at 35-3/8 inches, two mandatory head backbands at 37-5/8 inches, two side-stop blanks at 83 inches, and one head-stop blank at 26 inches. These are rough purchasing targets with the documented field-fit allowance already included. If an evidence-supported square head receives caps, reserve two M-204 blanks at 39-1/8 inches. Reject any board whose checks, knots, sapwood, bow, crook, twist, or color break enters a required clear lane; do not treat the contingency board as permission to accept poor stock.

## Stock selection and milling

1. Calculate linear footage by profile from the completed worksheet. Add at least 20 percent for color matching, end checks, scribing, and test pieces.
2. Select straight, quarter-sawn casing boards with compatible ray figure. Pair the two side casings and reserve the best matching board for the head.
3. Rough-crosscut parts with at least 2 inches of total length allowance.
4. Joint one face and one edge. Plane all members of one profile family together.
5. Mill casing to 3/4 inch and backband to 1 inch, then sticker and rest before final width.
6. Mill stops as a wide mother blank to 3/8 inch before ripping 1-1/4-inch strips. Do not hand-feed an unsupported narrow strip through the planer.
7. Rip final widths from the marked reference edge. Keep offcuts large enough for setup samples.
8. Ease visible arrises only after a labeled profile sample has been approved.
9. Mark part ID, face, room side, hinge or latch side, top, and bottom. Preserve the marks through dry fit, removal, finishing, and permanent installation.

## Profile operations

- Casing M-201: 3/4 x 4-1/2 inches. Keep the back flat. Ease both visible long arrises about 1/32 inch with a finely set block plane, scraper, or controlled abrasive block.
- Backband M-202: 1 x 1-1/8 inches. Its back shares the wall plane with the casing back; its front face stands 1/4 inch proud of the casing. Ease exposed arrises about 1/32 inch.
- Stop M-203: 3/8 x 1-1/4 inches. Ease only the exposed door-side arris about 1/16 inch; keep the jamb-bearing edge square.
- Head cap M-204: 7/8 x 1-1/2 inches. A restrained 1/8-inch chamfer on the exposed front arrises is the buildable default only after the sample agrees with adjacent original trim.
- Plinth M-205: 7/8 inch thick by field-verified width and height. Match the adjacent profile exactly. Do not infer a plinth from the availability of stock.

Clamp wide stock for plunge-router work and use an edge guide or template. Machine narrow profiles as wide mother blanks whenever possible. No router table, shaper, or CNC is required.

A dado blade is not required for any default moulding profile. Use a standard ripping or combination blade for straight stock preparation and a standard crosscut setup for lengths and joints. Where the casing back needs localized relief at a proud jamb, use a plunge router with an edge guide or controlled hand-plane and chisel work after recording the exact field condition.

## Wall and jamb fit

- Lay out the 3/16-inch reveal from the inside edge of the completed jamb, never from the drywall.
- A flat casing must bear without being pulled hollow by nails.
- For a locally proud jamb, transfer the projection to the casing back and cut only the shallow relief needed for the casing to sit flat. Preserve at least 1/2 inch of sound casing thickness at every relief.
- For a recessed jamb edge, use a continuous fitted wood shim or extension approved against the completed jamb. Do not bridge a void and force the casing inward.
- Stop and correct the substrate when an abrupt hump, loose plaster, or projection greater than 1/8 inch prevents full bearing. Do not hide a structural or moisture problem with trim.
- Scribe the wall-side edge only after the jamb-side reveal and joint are proven.

## Head-joint selection

### Mitered surround

Use when adjacent original openings have mitered casing.

1. Mark both side-casing inside short points at H-L + R and H-R + R.
2. Cut the top miters long, place the legs on the reveal lines, and transfer the actual head intersections.
3. Cut the head casing with an inside short point of W + 2R.
4. Tune the visible face on a shooting board until both miters close under light hand pressure.
5. Use a biscuit or 5 x 30 mm Domino only as concealed alignment after a scrap setup proves the mortise does not break through either face.

### Square-butt head

Use only when adjacent original openings support a square head, cap, or similar entablature.

1. Cut the side casings square to H-L + R and H-R + R from the selected bottom datum.
2. Fit the head across the tops of the legs at W + 2R + 2C.
3. Keep the head level while preserving the 3/16-inch reveal over the jamb.
4. Close each butt joint under light hand pressure. Optional concealed biscuits or Domino tenons are alignment aids, not permission to pull a poor joint closed.

## Dry-fit and label the door stop

1. Hang and fully fit the operating door before locating the stop.
2. Close and latch the door. Confirm uniform perimeter clearances and free latch operation.
3. Fit the head stop between the jamb legs. Keep its visible edge parallel to the door face.
4. Place 0.010- to 0.020-inch paper or card shims between the door and stop at the hinge, latch, and head reference points.
5. Fit each side stop from the bottom datum to the underside of the dry-fitted head stop.
6. Use the minimum temporary 18-gauge tacks needed to hold the stops. Close, latch, and reopen the door; adjust until contact is quiet and continuous without bowing the slab or preloading the latch.
7. Label the head and both sides with orientation and position marks, record the shim locations, pull the temporary tacks, and remove the stops for final P120 preparation and finish. Do not complete the permanent fastener pattern at this stage.

## Dry-fit and label the side casings

1. Make a rigid 3/16-inch reveal gauge and mark the head and both jamb legs.
2. Fit the bottom of each casing to the finished floor or verified plinth top. Scribe independently because the two sides may differ.
3. Hold each leg to its reveal line and transfer the actual head-joint marks.
4. Cut long, then trim or shoot to the line. Maintain the paired grain orientation.
5. Tack each leg at no more than three locations after the reveal remains within 1/32 inch and the casing bears flat. These tacks are temporary fitting aids, not the permanent fastener pattern.
6. Mark each leg's face, side, bottom datum, reveal line, and mating head joint. Keep the legs temporarily tacked only until the head, backband, and optional transitions have been dry-fitted.

## Dry-fit and label the head casing

1. Place the head blank over the tacked legs and transfer both joint locations from the actual work.
2. Cut the selected miter or square-butt configuration long.
3. Tune one end, return the head to the opening, and scribe the second end.
4. Dry-fit the complete three-piece casing set. The head must be level, both legs plumb, and both joints closed without clamp pressure.
5. Label the head, both mating joints, face, room side, and orientation. Mark any concealed alignment-aid locations, but make no permanent glue or nail connection.
6. Leave the casing set temporarily tacked only long enough to fit the backband, cap, plinth, baseboard interface, and scribes. Then pull the tacks and remove the complete labeled set for final P120 preparation and finish.

## Dry-fit and label the backband

1. Mill and approve the 1 x 1-1/8-inch profile sample. Confirm its face is 1/4 inch proud of the fitted casing.
2. Match the casing head-joint language before cutting: use a mitered backband around a mitered surround or the released square treatment around a square-butt head.
3. For a mitered face, rough-cut each side backband to its fitted casing outside long point + B + 2 inches. For a square face, rough-cut each side backband to its fitted casing length + C + 2 inches. In either scheme, rough-cut the head to fitted head-casing outside width + 2B + 2 inches; transfer all final cuts from the fitted assembly.
4. Dry-fit the three pieces. Keep the reveal of the casing edge consistent and close joints by hand.
5. Label every band with face, side, orientation, and mating-joint marks. Mark the future backband-to-casing glue land, but apply no glue and drive no permanent brads.
6. At a terminating end, dry-fit a self-return or scribe to the adjacent surface. Label the return and its glue face; keep the return dry through fitting, removal, final preparation, finishing, and full cure. Glue it only during post-cure permanent installation; never leave exposed end grain at eye level.
7. Remove the labeled backband with the casing set for final P120 preparation, finishing, and full cure.

## Optional head cap and plinth

- M-202C is mandatory even when no cap is used. Fit and label the complete three-piece backband before sizing any optional cap.
- Use the head cap only with a field-supported square-head treatment. Seat it atop the completed head backband. Cut its finished length to the actual fitted head-backband outside width plus 1-1/2 inches, producing 3/4-inch end overhangs; rough length is that finished target plus 2 inches total field-fit allowance.
- Dry-fit the 7/8 x 1-1/2-inch cap flat, level, and centered over M-202C. Label its face, orientation, centered position, and future cap-to-head-backband glue land, then remove it with the trim set. Do not glue or nail it before finish cure.
- Use plinth blocks only when adjacent original work contains them. The plinth must receive the full visible casing/backband composition or provide a deliberate backband termination above.
- Dry-fit and label each plinth and its baseboard interface. Without a plinth, dry-fit the baseboard directly to the casing/backband composition. Do not notch the new casing around the baseboard.

## Surface preparation and finish

1. Complete the full dry fit—including stops, casing, backband, optional cap/plinth, reliefs, scribes, returns, and baseboard interfaces—before finish preparation.
2. Confirm every part and mating joint is labeled, photograph the accepted dry fit, pull temporary tacks, and remove all trim from the opening.
3. Plane or scrape mill marks, then sand uniformly through P100 to a final P120 without rounding joints, erasing fitting marks, or changing the reveal edge.
4. Mask only the proven glue faces: casing-to-casing joints; backband-to-casing lands, corner joints, and self-returns; approved plinth-to-trim joints; and the approved cap-to-head-backband joint. Keep visible finish surfaces exposed. Do not create glue lands to the wall, floor, plaster, drywall, or jamb.
5. Make a labeled sample from the same stock and compare Rubio Monocoat Oil Plus 2C Pure with adjacent house millwork.
6. Vacuum all surfaces and use only the current Rubio-approved cleaning process; do not substitute mineral spirits. Mix Oil Plus 2C Part A:Part B 3:1 by volume or weight, spread one thin coat, allow the stated reaction period, and wipe away every trace of unbonded excess.
7. Finish all fitted parts off the opening and leave them undisturbed through the full cure interval. Current Rubio guidance identifies roughly 24–36 hours to dry and five days to full cure at room temperature; the current container/TDS and actual conditions control handling.
8. After full cure, remove masking from the glue faces, verify that they are clean and finish-free, and follow the permanent installation sequence. Fill holes with a color-matched repair and blend touch-up only on a sample-approved schedule.
9. Lay oily cloths flat outdoors to cure or submerge them in water in a sealed metal container for lawful disposal.

## Permanent installation and fastener map

Do not begin this sequence until every part has been dry-fitted, labeled, removed, final-sanded to P120, finished with the approved schedule, and allowed to reach full cure.

1. If field evidence supports plinths, install both cured plinths first at their recorded locations. They establish the casing bottom datum. Keep each masked plinth-to-trim glue land clean for the casing step; never glue a plinth to the floor or wall.
2. Reinstall the two side casings and the head on their recorded reveal and plinth-top or floor datums. Apply glue only to the masked casing-to-casing joint faces and any approved masked casing-to-plinth joint; close the already-proven joints by hand.
3. Pin the casing inner edges to the jamb, then fasten the outer zones to verified framing. Work from the head joints outward while rechecking reveal, plumb, level, and full bearing.
4. Reinstall the mandatory backband, including every labeled self-return. Apply a thin glue film only to the masked backband-to-casing lands, corner joints, and self-returns; never glue to plaster, drywall, jamb, floor, or wall. Drive the 18-gauge brads only after projection and joints pass.
5. Reinstall any approved cap atop the completed M-202C head backband. Glue only the masked cap-to-head-backband joint and use the released concealed or top-driven fastener locations.
6. Reinstall the head stop and then the side stops last at their labels and recorded shim references. Tack, cycle and latch the door, confirm the proven contact, remove the shims, and only then complete the stop fastener pattern.
7. Set fasteners without crushing fibers. Fill holes with the sample-approved repair material and perform only the approved local touch-up.

| Location | Permanent fastener and spacing | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Casing inner edge | 18-gauge brads into jamb, 12 to 16 inches on center | Minimum 3/4 inch into sound jamb wood; keep clear of the reveal edge and stop fasteners |
| Casing outer zone | 15-gauge finish nails into verified framing, about 16 inches on center | Minimum 1 inch into verified framing after subtracting the actual casing, wall finish, shim, and void stack |
| Backband | 18-gauge brads angled into casing, 8 to 12 inches on center | Minimum 1/2 inch into casing without point exit or splitting; glue only to casing and offset from casing nails |
| Door stop | 18-gauge brads into jamb, 10 to 12 inches on center | Minimum 3/4 inch into sound jamb wood; tack first and complete only after the door is cycled |
| Miter or butt joint | Optional 18-gauge pinning or concealed alignment aid | Joint must close before the fastener is driven |

For every fastener family, calculate required length from the actual installed layer stack and record the arithmetic on the field worksheet. Build a matching offcut stack and prove the selected standard fastener produces the required sound-wood embedment without point exit, edge split, surface damage, or collision with another fastener path. Hold installation if no available standard length passes the offcut proof. Keep the nailer in sequential-fire mode, keep fingers outside the projected fastener path, and disconnect the air supply or battery before adjustment or jam clearing. Verify framing and utility locations before driving long fasteners.

## Final quality control

- Casing reveal is 3/16 inch with no local variation greater than 1/32 inch.
- Miter and butt-joint gaps are less than 0.010 inch; adjacent faces are flush within 0.010 inch.
- Head casing is level within 1/16 inch across the opening; side casings are plumb within 1/16 inch over their full length.
- Backband face projects 1/4 inch beyond the casing, varying no more than 1/32 inch.
- Wall-side scribe gaps are no greater than 1/32 inch at normal viewing distance.
- Nail heads are set without crushed fibers, split edges, or visible utility damage.
- Grain and ray figure form a deliberate pair; no end checks or sapwood interrupt a joint.
- Finish color and sheen match the approved sample.
- The door swings freely, latches without lifting or pushing, and contacts the stop without springing or rattle.
- Optional cap, plinth, and baseboard transitions agree with adjacent original trim.

## Stop conditions

- Stop if the number of trimmed faces, head-joint scheme, cap, or plinth treatment is not signed off.
- Stop if the casing cannot bear flat without being pulled into position by nails.
- Stop if any joint needs clamp pressure to hide a layout or cutting error.
- Stop if a routed relief would leave less than 1/2 inch of casing thickness.
- Stop if a finish sample has not been approved against the actual room trim.
- Stop if both jamb legs have not been recorded plumb in both planes, the head has not been recorded level, both diagonals/square have not been checked, or winding sticks show unresolved jamb-face twist.
- Stop if fastener length has not been calculated from the actual layer stack and proven on a matching offcut, or if no standard length provides the specified embedment without exit, split, or utility risk.
- Stop if any casing, backband, cap, plinth, or stop is about to be permanently glued or nailed before the complete dry fit is labeled and removed, final P120 preparation is complete, and the applied finish has reached full cure.
- Stop if the door does not hang, swing, latch, and close correctly before the stops are installed.

## Essential safety

Use the machine maker's current instructions, guards, dust extraction, eye protection, hearing protection, and suitable respiratory protection. Support long stock at infeed and outfeed. Use push blocks, featherboards, and purpose-built jigs for ripping or profiling narrow work. Clamp plunge-router work; keep both hands on the router until the cutter stops. Disconnect nailers before adjustment or jam clearing. Do not use compressed air to clean white-oak dust from clothing or the shop.
